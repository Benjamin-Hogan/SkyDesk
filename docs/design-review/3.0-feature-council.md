# SkyDesk 3.0: feature council (consensus)

> Three members argued over 3 rounds to agree on **exactly 3 major features** that integrate with the
> weather, card, map and radar screens:
> - **Juno:** product and experience advocate
> - **Rex:** firmware and systems skeptic
> - **Claude:** moderator and integration architect
>
> Result: **3-0 ACCEPT** on 2026-09-25. This document records ideas and decisions only. Each screen
> change still goes through the Mr Stacks mock review (3 rounds, ≥ 7) before any firmware is written.

## The decision

| # | Feature | In one line | Integrates with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Today's Sky** | An SD log of every overhead pass: "passed 2 min ago" and a Today page | weather chip, clock tap, card, and the Heads-up gate |
| 2 | **Setup portal** | A WiFi hotspot + iPhone page for WiFi, location and facing. It is also the recovery path | boot error screen (QR), Settings |
| 3 | **Heads-up** | "Southwest 737 · overhead in ~40 s" on the screen you're actually looking at, plus an amber LED | weather chip, radar strip, map focus, LED |

**Build order:** Today → Portal → Heads-up.
- Today has to log a week of will-pop promises before Heads-up's amber can ship.
- The portal fills that week and lands the recovery path early. Serial RX is dead, so today a wrong
  WiFi password means a USB re-flash.
- Heads-up's code can land behind its flag during the same window.

Both Juno and Rex proposed this order independently in round 3.

## 1. Today's Sky
- **Model:** a pure `spotter.cpp`, host-tested, fed by the tracker's ENTER/EXIT events.
  - Records hex, type, operator, route, time, peak elevation and minimum distance.
  - **Never owner names.**
- **Storage:**
  - Daily append files on SD, using the radar's `.tmp` → rename pattern.
  - Writes happen under `sdLock` and are flushed by `netTask` between jobs, never during a radar
    convert.
  - Running totals live in NVS, so the counts survive without a card.
- **Surfaces:**
  - The weather chip reads `Passed 2 min ago · SWA 737 ›` for 10 minutes after a pop (the M7 wording).
  - Tapping the clock opens a **Today page**: `31 overhead · 212 nearby`, a 24-bar hourly histogram
    (the PHX east/west flow flips show up in it), and the rarest type of the day.
- **Budget:** about 1.6 KB of RAM (a 1 KB daily-unique bitset, the hourly bars, the rarest record
  and a record ring). Paid back by cutting `ROUTE_CACHE_N` from 16 to 8.
  - **Condition (Rex):** measure the payback as **8-bit** free and largest block, at rest and at
    the TLS peak.
- **Card replay:** a tap on the "passed" chip shows the frozen, dimmed card.
  - Allowed only with a host test proving that a real pop preempts a replay.
  - Its roughly 300 B frozen Aircraft + RouteInfo must fit the budget.
  - Otherwise, or if the payback falls short, it moves automatically to 3.1.
- **Deferred:** the `First A350 here` card line. The card layout came out of review, so it gets
  no unreviewed line.
- **Also logs** the Heads-up promises against their outcomes (see 3).

## 2. Setup portal
- **Mode:** a separate boot mode with softAP, DNSServer and the core's sync `WebServer`.
  - It never runs alongside TLS, so it costs **0 RAM** in the normal boot. No async libraries.
  - Settings are saved to NVS, then the device reboots.
- **Entry:**
  - From Settings, or after N minutes of WiFi failure.
  - The boot error screen then shows a **WiFi-join QR**, so one scan with the iPhone camera joins
    `SkyDesk-Setup`.
- **WiFi:** SSID and password.
- **Location:**
  - A `lat,lon` paste, checked by a **nudge gate** of about 1 px at the finest map zoom around the
    build centre.
  - Outside the gate, the location is saved, but map and radar say `map built for Gilbert` instead
    of drawing a wrong map.
  - **Condition (Rex):** the gate check is host-tested.
  - An optional static HTTPS helper page that shows your `lat,lon`, only if the owner chooses to
    host one.
- **Facing:** a compass rose plus a degrees box. The on-device dial stays.
- **Why not "phone GPS + phone compass":**
  - iOS Safari gives Geolocation and DeviceOrientation only to secure (HTTPS) pages.
  - `http://192.168.4.1` in a captive-portal sheet isn't one.
  - The two agents both raised this independently.
- **Out of 3.0:**
  - **ZIP/city geocoding:** a centroid is miles off and fails the gate anyway.
  - **SD basemap packs:** 3.1. The basemaps (4 × 38.4 KB) and the clutter mask are baked in at
    build time.
  - **The "Is it NE of you?" card check.**

## 3. Heads-up
- **Surfaces:**
  - When a plane will pop, the weather chip and the radar strip read
    `Southwest 737 · overhead in ~40 s ›` in amber. Amber still means the plane that is about to
    take the screen.
  - Tapping it opens the map focused on that plane, with its leader and pop square.
- **LED:** the RGB LED gets **one meaning**: amber breathing = inbound, and a double pulse on the
  pop. There is no rain colour, because an LED with two meanings means neither. No speaker.
- **Compute:**
  - Runs on core 1 from the shared `Traffic` (`mapWillPopSecs` and the `mapPickFocus` hold and
    2-miss rule).
  - Interpolated with `millis()` between the existing **5 s** polls.
  - **No new poll cadence:** every poll is a fresh TLS handshake, and a hot 2 s poll would starve
    radar backfill.
- **`chipMessage()` arbiter:** pure and host-tested; it is the shared rule for the weather chip.
  - Priority: `[warning, reserved for 3.1 NWS]` > will-pop > passed (10 min) > nearby.
  - The rain cue keeps its own slot.
- **Gate:** the amber text **and** the amber LED sit behind one config flag. It flips on only
  when the Today log shows **≥ 80 % of promises kept over a week**.
  - A promise is **kept** if the plane pops within ±15 s, or ±30 % of the lead time (whichever is
    larger), of the **first** promise made for that hex.
  - A promise dropped by the 2-miss rule is **broken**.
  - A promise is **excluded**, not broken, if it was live while ADS-B was failing (`failStreak > 0`)
    or during a provider failover, or if its hex was dismissed. The log reports the excluded count,
    so the gate can't be passed by excluding (Rex).
  - `spotter.cpp` computes this, host-tested.
  - Under 80 %: tune the will-pop sampling; don't ship the amber.

## Guardrails for all three
- Measure 8-bit RAM (`heap_caps_*(MALLOC_CAP_8BIT)`) at rest and at the TLS peak before flashing.
  Keep ≥ 60 KB free at rest.
- One TLS session at a time, and no new TLS hosts in 3.0.
- Run the 2 strong reviewer agents before re-flashing after any regression.
- Update `screens.py` first, then the firmware. The Mr Stacks 3-round review applies.

## How it went

| | Round 1 top 3 | Round 2 vote | Round 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude | Portal, Spotter log, Night/ambient | Portal, Today, Heads-up (withdrew Night/ambient) | proposed the draft |
| Juno | Heads-up, Today's Sky, Portal | Portal, Today, Heads-up | ACCEPT + defined the gate |
| Rex | Portal, NWS alerts, Spotter log | Portal, Today, NWS | ACCEPT + build order + gate exclusions |

**Portal and Today were unanimous from round 1. The fight was over slot 3:**
- **Rex, for NWS:** it's the only candidate that adds information the device lacks: heat, haboob
  dust (not a precipitation echo) and flash floods downstream.
- **Juno, against NWS:** Phoenix runs Extreme Heat Warnings for weeks, so a heat banner becomes
  wallpaper. Only the short-fuse warnings are worth showing.
- **Moderator fact check:** it removed Juno's payload objection. The api.weather.gov chain is
  RSA-2048 Let's Encrypt (about 5 KB), lighter than Open-Meteo's P-521, and a quiet reply is 230 B.
- **Outcome:** Heads-up won slot 3 (2–1).
  - Rex's conditions (5 s + interpolation, the 80 % gate, the arbiter) were adopted in full.
  - The arbiter reserves the top slot so NWS plugs in at 3.1 with no UI rework.

**Moderator's correction during the debate:** I first said `net_task.cpp:191` sets a hot 2 s
cadence. It only defers radar. ADS-B stays at 5 s on weather and 2 s on the plane screen.

## 3.1 queue
0. **Radar forecast, next hour** (owner request, 2026-09-25): add 4 HRRR forecast frames
   (+15/+30/+45/+60 min) after the observed loop.
   - Source: IEM `cgi-bin/wms/hrrr/refd.cgi`, layers `refd_0000`..`refd_NNNN` in 15-min steps.
     It's keyless and returns the same uncompressed TIFF as n0q.
   - Colours: the same colour table, verified with 0 misses over 6,425 px across the country.
   - Metadata: `data/gis/images/4326/hrrr/refd_NNNN.json` (`model_init_utc`). IEM's newest run
     lags about 3 h, so "now + 60 min" means about F240 of that run.
   - Needs: a clear `FORECAST` treatment (a model guess, not radar); no clutter cleaning on model
     frames; about 1 extra minute of downloads per refresh. Mr Stacks review first.
1. **NWS alerts** (api.weather.gov, keyless, User-Agent) in the arbiter's warning slot. Heat vs
   short-fuse filtering gets settled then.
2. **SD basemap packs + geocoding**, so the location can move anywhere.
3. **Card replay**, if it was deferred.
4. **ISS passes** on the sky dome (TLE + SGP4).

## Cut
- **Night/ambient as a feature.** `LDR_AUTO` is a config flag plus a day of LDR logging, and the
  CYD's LDR is biased. Quiet hours would break "the pop always wins".
- **Plane photos:** JPEG RAM and licensing.
- **Speaker:** nothing is wired to the header, and I2S DMA costs RAM.
- **BLE provisioning:** 50+ KB of RAM, and an iPhone has no Web Bluetooth.
- **An always-on web server:** permanent sockets starve TLS.
- **mDNS back on**, or any second TLS session.
- **"Move anywhere" location** that ignores the baked basemaps.
